Privacy policy

Effective {{effective_date}}

ECHO is a private daily practice for the mind. This policy says, in plain words, what ECHO keeps on your phone, what leaves it and when, who handles it for us, and the choices you have.

The short version

  • Your journal lives on your phone. Entries, recordings and pattern cards are encrypted there. There is no account and no cloud copy.
  • Your voice and your entries leave your phone only after you agree, and only to OpenAI, to become words or to be read for pattern cards. OpenAI does not train on them.
  • ECHO’s own server keeps none of your voice or words. It keeps a random install id and a count of voice minutes, so the free allowance and ECHO Plus work without an account.
  • ECHO Plus is bought through Google Play. RevenueCat keeps the record of your subscription for us.
  • ECHO has no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting, and does not track you across other apps or websites.

1. Who we are

ECHO is made by {{company_legal_name}} (“we”, “us”). We are responsible for the personal data described in this policy. You can reach us at {{support_email}}, or by post at {{company_address}}.

2. What stays on your phone

Almost everything you put into ECHO stays on your phone:

  • Your entries, including the mood you chose, the words made from your voice, and your recordings.
  • Your morning intentions, your pattern cards, and how you felt after a breathing practice.
  • Your answers in ECHO’s first steps, and your settings, such as your reminder time and theme.

Your entries, recordings, the words made from them, your answers in the first steps and your pattern cards are encrypted on your phone, using a 256 bit AES key that is made on your phone and kept in Android’s secure storage. The key never leaves your phone, and we never see it.

Android’s own backup is turned off for ECHO, so your journal is not copied to your Google account. There is no ECHO account and no cloud copy. This also means that if your phone is lost or reset, or you uninstall ECHO without exporting first, your journal cannot be recovered by anyone, including us.

If you turn on the lock, ECHO asks for your fingerprint, face or phone passcode before it opens. Android checks these; ECHO never sees them. While the lock is on, Android shows a blank preview of ECHO in your recent apps and blocks screenshots of it.

3. What leaves your phone, and when

Your voice, after you agree

The first time you speak an entry, ECHO asks whether your voice may be turned into words, and names OpenAI as the service that does it. If you choose Keep my voice only, your recordings stay on your phone and are never sent. You can change your choice at any time in You.

If you agree:

  • After you stop recording, your recording is sent over an encrypted connection to ECHO’s voice server, which passes it to OpenAI’s transcription service and sends the words back. A long recording is sent in parts. OpenAI’s transcription service does not store your recording and does not train on it. ECHO’s voice server holds the recording and the words in memory only while it handles the request, and keeps nothing.
  • If you choose to see your words as you speak, ECHO asks you again first. Your voice then streams from your phone straight to OpenAI’s live transcription service, using a temporary key from ECHO’s voice server that must be used within 60 seconds. OpenAI may keep what is sent and received for up to 30 days to check for abuse, then deletes it. It is not used for training.
  • If you speak Hindi, or turn on Tidy my words, the finished text also goes through a second step at OpenAI, through ECHO’s voice server, to write Hindi in Roman script or to tidy the words. OpenAI may keep that text for up to 30 days to check for abuse, then deletes it. It is not used for training.

Your entries, when you ask for pattern cards

Pattern cards are made only when you ask for them, and ECHO asks for your agreement first, separately from voice. When you ask:

  • ECHO sends your newest entries, at most 30, and the first 6,000 characters of each, with their dates, over an encrypted connection to ECHO’s server, which passes them to OpenAI to be read.
  • Before anything goes to OpenAI, ECHO’s server checks the entries for language about suicide or self harm. If it finds any, nothing is sent to OpenAI, no cards are made, and ECHO shows you where to find support.
  • OpenAI does not train on your entries. It may keep them for up to 30 days to check for abuse, then deletes them. ECHO’s server keeps none of them.
  • The cards come back to your phone and are kept there, encrypted. You can withdraw your agreement, or delete the cards, at any time in Patterns.

Reports about a pattern card

If you report a card, ECHO sends us only the reason you chose, which idea the card drew on, and which model made it. It never sends the card’s text or your words.

Your install id

The first time ECHO needs it, your phone makes a random install id and keeps it in Android’s secure storage. It is not made from your phone’s hardware or from any account, it names no one, and it holds nothing you wrote or said.

ECHO sends it with each request to ECHO’s voice server, so the server can count your free minutes of voice into words and know whether you have ECHO Plus. ECHO also sends it to RevenueCat, so your subscription is attached to your phone without an account. For each install id, ECHO’s server keeps how many minutes of voice have become words this month and whether ECHO Plus is active. It keeps nothing you said.

Delete everything leaves the install id in place, so that a subscription you paid for, and the free minutes you have used, stay attached to your phone. Uninstalling ECHO removes it, and a new install makes a new one.

Purchases

ECHO Plus is sold through Google Play. Google handles your payment, and we never see your card or bank details. Google’s privacy policy applies to that payment.

We use RevenueCat to manage subscriptions. When you start a trial, buy, renew or restore ECHO Plus, RevenueCat receives the purchase record from Google Play, such as the plan, price, currency, dates and whether it renews, linked to your install id. RevenueCat also receives your phone’s locale and currency, to show the right prices. We use these records to give you ECHO Plus and to count our subscription revenue.

Internet addresses and server logs

When your phone talks to ECHO’s voice server, the server sees your internet (IP) address, as any web service does. It holds the address in memory for up to ten minutes to limit how many requests one address can make, and does not write it to its logs. Our host, Vercel, may keep short technical logs of requests, which can include the address, under its own policies.

ECHO’s server writes one short line for each request: which service was used, whether it worked, how large the request was and how long it took. It never logs your voice, your words, or anything worked out from them, such as the language you spoke or whether a support message was shown.

When you write to us

If you email us, we receive your email address and what you write, and use them only to answer you. ECHO never attaches your entries to an email.

4. What ECHO does not do

  • No ads, and no advertising id.
  • No analytics, crash reporting or tracking software, and no tracking across other apps or websites.
  • No access to your location, contacts, photos, camera or health data.
  • No selling or sharing of your personal data for advertising.
  • No training of AI models on your voice or words, by us or by OpenAI.

5. Reminders

Reminders are scheduled on your phone and come from your phone. ECHO uses no push service, so no server knows when your reminders are or sends them. A reminder never holds anything you wrote: the daily one says “A small space is waiting for you.”, and if you start a free trial, one reminder two days before it ends says “Your ECHO Plus trial ends in 2 days.”

6. Who handles data for us

We use a few providers. Each handles your data only to provide its service to us, and none of them uses it for advertising.

  • OpenAI turns your voice into words, tidies words when you ask, and reads entries for pattern cards. It receives your recordings, the words made from them and the entries you send, only after you agree.
  • Vercel hosts ECHO’s voice server. Requests to that server pass through it, including your internet address.
  • RevenueCat manages ECHO Plus subscriptions. It receives your install id and your purchase records.
  • Google distributes ECHO through Google Play and takes payment for ECHO Plus, under its own privacy policy.

OpenAI, Vercel and RevenueCat process data in the United States, and may process it in other countries. Where the law requires it, we rely on safeguards such as standard contractual clauses for these transfers.

7. How long data is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete it, use Delete everything, or uninstall ECHO.
  • ECHO’s server: your voice and words only while a request is handled. The minutes count for an install id for as long as it is needed to apply the monthly allowance and to stop abuse.
  • OpenAI: the transcription service keeps nothing. Words shown as you speak, the second step for Hindi or Tidy my words, and entries read for pattern cards may be kept for up to 30 days to check for abuse, then deleted.
  • RevenueCat and Google: purchase records for as long as needed to provide your subscription and to meet legal duties, such as keeping tax records.
  • Emails to us: for as long as we need them to help you.

8. Your choices and rights

You can decide, in the app, at any time:

  • Whether your voice is turned into words, or kept only on your phone, in You.
  • Whether words appear as you speak, and whether Tidy my words is on.
  • Whether ECHO may send your entries for pattern cards, and whether to keep the cards, in Patterns.
  • Whether reminders and the lock are on.
  • To export your journal, or delete all data from your phone, in You, under Your data.

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct or delete your personal data, to receive a copy of it, to object to or restrict how it is used, and to withdraw your consent at any time. Most of your data is only on your phone, where you control it directly. For the little that we or our providers hold, write to {{support_email}}. So that we can find purchase records that belong to you, include your Google Play order number, which is in the receipt Google emailed you. We will answer within one month. You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live.

Where the GDPR applies, we rely on your consent to turn your voice into words and to read your entries; on our contract with you to provide ECHO Plus and keep the install id and purchase records; and on our legitimate interest in keeping the service secure for internet addresses and server logs.

If you are in India, the person who answers questions about your data on our behalf is {{grievance_officer_name}}, at {{support_email}}.

9. Children

ECHO is for adults, 18 and over. It is not made for children, and we do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. A journal kept by a child is only on their phone and can be removed there. If you believe a child has used ECHO, write to us and we will delete any records we hold for that install.

10. Security

Your journal is encrypted on your phone with AES, using a 256 bit key in GCM mode, held in Android’s secure storage. Everything that leaves your phone travels over encrypted connections. ECHO’s servers keep no journal content, so there is none there to lose. No system is perfectly secure, which is why we hold as little as we can.

11. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will post the new version here with a new effective date. If a change affects what leaves your phone, ECHO will ask for your agreement again in the app before it sends anything under the new terms.
