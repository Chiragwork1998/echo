The same thoughts, running the same day again.

You know the feeling. The worry from Monday is back on Thursday, in different clothes, and your body feels it before you can name it.

ECHO gives you a few quiet minutes a day to settle your body, say what is true out loud, and keep every word. Over time it shows you what keeps returning, in your own words.

What ECHO is not

  • Not a chatbot. Nothing talks back at you.
  • Not a library of content to get through.
  • Not therapy, diagnosis or emergency care.

Breathe

What are you feeling?

Breathe asks that one question and answers with a short practice made for it: a breath that shifts your body, and one move for your mind. Close your eyes. Sound and a calm voice lead the way.

Help for panic, anxiety and hurt is never behind a paywall.

Settle uses cyclic sighing. In a 2023 Stanford study, five minutes a day of cyclic sighing lifted mood more than mindfulness meditation. Balban et al., Cell Reports Medicine, 2023.

  • Anxious

    Settle, 3 min Free

    Two breaths in through the nose, one long breath out. At the end, name the feeling to yourself in one word.

    Balban et al., Stanford, cyclic sighing. Dan Siegel, name it to tame it.

  • Panicky

    Ground, 3 min Free

    Long, slow breaths out, as if through a straw. Your eyes find things around you, your feet find the floor, and you are reminded that the wave passes.

    Peter Levine and Deb Dana, orienting.

  • Hurting

    Soothe, 4 min Free

    A hand on your heart, slow breaths with a longer breath out, and three kind sentences to yourself.

    Kristin Neff, the self compassion break.

  • Wanting to feel grateful

    Heart, 5 min Free at 3 and 5 min

    About five and a half slow breaths a minute, as if through your heart. At the halfway bell, a moment of gratitude or love, felt again.

    HeartMath Institute, Quick Coherence. Joe Dispenza, elevated emotion.

  • Angry

    Cool, 3 min Plus

    You do not have to answer yet. In through the nose, then a long breath out with a quiet hum, lips closed.

    Maxwell Maltz, the delayed reaction. Bhramari, the humming breath of yoga.

  • Overwhelmed

    One thing, 4 min Plus

    Box breathing, four counts each. The voice helps you picture a small quiet room inside you, and rest there.

    Mark Divine, box breathing. Maxwell Maltz, the quiet room.

  • Can’t sleep

    Sleep, 10 min Plus

    Four rounds of in for 4, hold for 7, out for 8. Then long breaths out while the voice fades and the screen goes dark.

    Andrew Weil, the relaxing breath.

  • Low or flat

    Lift, 3 min Plus

    Sit up. Fuller breaths in and even breaths out, a little brighter each round. Then notice one small thing that is still okay.

    Stephen Porges and Deb Dana, moving gently out of shutdown, and glimmers.

  • Before something hard

    Ready, 4 min Plus

    Two minutes of box breathing. Then remember a time you did something hard and it went well, until you can feel that feeling again now.

    Maxwell Maltz, the winning feeling.

  • A craving or an urge

    Ride the wave, 3 min Plus

    Slow breaths while you watch the urge rise, peak and fall like a wave, without acting on it.

    G. Alan Marlatt, urge surfing.

With ECHO Plus every practice opens at any length, 3, 5, 10 or 20 minutes, and Heart at your own pace. If you feel dizzy or unwell, stop and breathe normally.

Speak

Say it the way it comes.

Answer one question out loud for seven minutes, over a living mountain sky. Or write, if that feels easier today. ECHO remembers which you chose.

Your exact words are kept, ums and changes of mind included. Keep them hidden so it is only you and the mountains, or, with ECHO Plus, watch them appear as you speak and have them tidied if you like.

Speak English, Hindi, or both. Hindi is written in Roman script, the way you would text it.

Your recording is always kept, encrypted on your phone. Turning it into words needs a connection, and ECHO asks before your voice leaves the phone.

Become

Begin the day as who you mean to be.

Mornings ask a different question. Arrive takes two minutes of slow breathing, then helps you see yourself in one ordinary moment of the day ahead, being the person you are becoming.

When you have answered, your own words come back over the sky, one phrase at a time, and stay while you feel them as if they are already true. Then go and live it.

Arrive draws on Maxwell Maltz, the theater of the mind, and Joe Dispenza, mental rehearsal. Mornings are free.

See

See what keeps returning.

After five reflections, ECHO can read your latest entries and name what keeps returning, in a few quiet cards. Every quote on a card is checked, word for word, against what you said.

Each card offers a practice of under two minutes and one question to sit with. It is a reflection from your own words, never a diagnosis or advice, and you can report any card that feels wrong.

Cards draw on ideas from Joe Dispenza, Stephen Porges, Deb Dana, Dan Siegel, James Pennebaker, Kristin Neff, Richard Schwartz, Gabor Maté, Tara Brach and Peter Levine. ECHO asks before it sends your entries to be read, and your first reading is free.

About ten minutes, once a day.

The practice is small on purpose. It fits before work, after the day, or anywhere you can close your eyes.

  1. 3 minutes

    Breathe

    Say what you are feeling and close your eyes. Sound and a calm voice lead the practice.

  2. 7 minutes

    Speak

    Answer one question out loud, or write. Your words are encrypted the moment they are saved.

  3. After 5 reflections

    See

    Read your echoes back, and ask ECHO what keeps returning when you are ready.

What stays, and what leaves.

ECHO is built so your journal lives on your phone. This is everything that happens to your words, in plain language.

Stays on your phone, encrypted

  • Every entry, recording and pattern card, sealed with a key kept in your phone’s secure storage.
  • Android backup is turned off for ECHO, so your journal is not copied to your Google account.
  • There is no account, and no cloud copy.

Leaves only when you agree

  • Your voice, to become words. It goes to OpenAI through ECHO’s voice server, or straight to OpenAI if you choose to see words as you speak.
  • Your latest entries, when you ask for pattern cards.
  • OpenAI does not train on them. Some may be kept by OpenAI for up to 30 days to check for abuse, then deleted. ECHO’s own server keeps none of your voice or words.

Never collected

  • No name, email or password.
  • No ads, no analytics, no crash reporting.
  • No location, contacts or photos.

What ECHO’s server does keep

  • A random install id that names no one, and how many voice minutes it has used this month, so free minutes and ECHO Plus work without an account.

Questions, answered plainly.

Is ECHO therapy?

No. ECHO is wellness software for a short daily practice. It is not therapy, diagnosis or emergency care, and pattern cards are reflections from your own words, never advice. If you are struggling, ECHO lists free helplines in You, and our support page has them too.

Who can read my journal?

You, on your phone. Entries and recordings are encrypted there with a key kept in your phone’s secure storage. There is no ECHO account and no cloud copy. Turn on the lock and ECHO asks for your fingerprint, face or passcode before it opens.

What leaves my phone, and does OpenAI train on it?

Only what you agree to. Your voice goes to OpenAI to become words, and your latest entries go to OpenAI when you ask for pattern cards. OpenAI does not train on them. Some of it may be kept by OpenAI for up to 30 days to check for abuse, then deleted. ECHO’s own server keeps none of your voice or words. The privacy policy has every detail.

What happens in a breathing practice?

You say what you are feeling, choose a length, and close your eyes. A soft swell of sound rises as you breathe in and falls as you breathe out, and a calm synthetic voice speaks the few moments that need words. The screen dims almost to black. You can turn the voice or the sound off, and the practices work offline.

Does ECHO work offline?

Yes. You can breathe, write, record your voice and read your echoes with no connection. Turning your voice into words and reading your patterns need one, and your recording is kept safely until then.

Which languages can I speak?

ECHO’s screens are in English. You can speak English, Hindi, or move between them. Hindi is written in Roman script, the way you would text it.

What is free, and what does ECHO Plus add?

Free includes unlimited writing, 60 minutes a month of voice into words, the practices for when you feel anxious, panicky or hurt, Heart at 3 and 5 minutes, Arrive in the mornings, your first pattern card reading, and export, lock and reminders. ECHO Plus adds unlimited voice into words with fair use of 20 hours a month, words as you speak, Tidy my words, every pattern card reading, and all ten practices at any length, with Heart at your own pace.

How do the free trial and cancelling work?

The yearly plan starts with 7 days free. You won’t be charged until the trial ends, and ECHO will remind you two days before. Cancel anytime in Google Play, under Payments & subscriptions. Plus stays until the end of the time you paid for.

Can I export or delete everything?

Yes. Export puts every entry and recording in one file that you share wherever you choose. Delete everything removes all that ECHO keeps on your phone, including the encryption key, and cannot be undone. A random install id stays behind so a subscription you paid for is not lost. Uninstalling ECHO removes it.

Where do the ideas in ECHO come from?

From writers, teachers and researchers such as Joe Dispenza, Maxwell Maltz, Kristin Neff, Peter Levine, Deb Dana and Tara Brach, and from a 2023 Stanford study of cyclic sighing. ECHO puts each idea in its own words and credits it by name. None of them has reviewed or endorsed ECHO.

What is early access, and is there an iPhone version?

Early access is a closed test of ECHO on Google Play before its public launch. Join with the Google account on your Android phone, install ECHO from Google Play, and leave the test whenever you like. ECHO is Android first. The iPhone version follows once it has been tested on iPhone.

Who is ECHO for?

Adults, 18 and over, who want a few private minutes a day to settle, speak and notice. ECHO is not made for children.
